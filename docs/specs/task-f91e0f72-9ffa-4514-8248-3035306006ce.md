# The poster frame is taken at a fixed one second, whatever is there

A video's thumbnail is a single frame grabbed at a hardcoded one-second offset, falling back to
the very first frame if that fails. For a clip that opens on darkness — a night scene, a fade-in,
a lens cap, a camera still finding focus — that frame is black, and the library shows a black
rectangle where a picture should be.

This is not hypothetical. On the staging instance a night-time firework fountain clip renders as
a solid black tile in the grid, while its own scrub-preview sprite shows the fountain clearly
from roughly five percent of the way in. The information needed to pick a better frame is already
being generated.

## Requirements

- Choosing the poster frame stops being "whatever is at one second". Sample a handful of
  candidate frames spread across the clip and pick one that actually shows something — reject
  frames that are near-uniform (almost all one colour, black or white alike) and prefer a frame
  with real tonal spread. Keep it cheap: a few frames, decoded small, not a scan of the clip.
- A clip that is genuinely dark or uniform throughout must still get a poster. The rule is "the
  best of the candidates", never "no thumbnail" — and never an error that fails the upload.
- Very short clips must not break the sampling: a two-second clip has to work, and there is one
  on the staging instance.
- The choice must be repeatable for videos already in the library: regenerating a video's
  thumbnails re-picks the poster with the new rule, so existing black tiles can be repaired
  without re-uploading. Confirm the existing per-photo thumbnail rebuild does this, and that the
  library-wide thumbnail backfill can be pointed at videos.
- Record what the rule is and why in the thumbnail documentation, including the cost — how many
  frames are decoded per video and roughly what that adds to ingesting a clip.

## Verification

This is a visual defect, so verify it visually, not only by unit test. Regenerate the thumbnails
of a dark-opening clip, fetch the resulting poster and confirm it is no longer near-black — a
histogram or mean-luminance check on the produced JPEG is enough evidence, and cheaper than
eyeballing. Report the before and after for at least one real clip.

## Tests

Unit tests over the frame-choosing rule with synthetic candidates: an all-black first frame and a
usable later one; every candidate uniform; a single-frame clip; a clip shorter than the sampling
window.

## Out of scope

- Letting the user choose the poster frame by hand from the scrub preview. That is a good idea and
  a separate piece of work; this task is about the automatic choice being sane by default.
- Animated previews on hover in the grid.
