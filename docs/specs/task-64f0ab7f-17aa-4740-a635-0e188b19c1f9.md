# A face marker has to show the reader which face is being named

On a group photograph the markers drawn over detected faces are too small to identify.
Measured on a concert photograph with 18 detected faces on the live instance: the smallest
marker rendered about 8 x 9 CSS pixels with a 2 px border. The faces panel beside it asks the
reader to name "Nepojmenovaný obličej 12", and there is no way to tell which of the 18 people
that is — so naming anyone on a photo of a crowd is guesswork.

The markers do render and do take clicks; a separate stacking defect that made them invisible
entirely was already fixed. This task is only about making them legible.

## Requirements

- A marker is always identifiable on the photograph: give it a minimum visual size and hit
  area, so it never renders as a handful of pixels however small the detected face is.
- Pointing at a face row in the side panel highlights the matching marker on the photograph,
  and pointing at a marker highlights its row. Both directions, and focus counts as pointing so
  the keyboard and touch work too.
- When the selected face is too small to recognise in place, show the reader an enlarged crop of
  that face — for example an inset beside the marker, or in the naming panel — so they can see
  who they are about to name. The full-size photograph is already on screen here, so the crop
  costs no extra download.
- Make the selected marker unmistakable against a busy photograph by dimming the rest, the way
  the review game already does. **Reuse that treatment rather than inventing a second one:**
  `web/src/components/review/review.css` does it with a single `box-shadow` on
  `.review-photo__box` — a huge spread of translucent black clipped by the photo container —
  and `ReviewStage` positions the box with `faceBoxStyle`/`padBbox` from `lib/faceGeometry`.
  Both are already shared components.
- Keep the existing rule that only the active marker carries a name label; drawing every name at
  once piles labels on top of each other on a group photo.
- Works on a phone as well as a desktop, and respects reduced-motion preferences.

## Edge cases

- Faces close enough together that enlarged markers would overlap.
- A marker near the edge of the frame, where an inset or label must not be clipped.
- A photograph with one large face, where the treatment must not look heavy-handed.
- A rotated photograph — the boxes already map through the rotation; keep that working.
