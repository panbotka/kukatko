# A pass over the slideshow player itself

The slideshow is the one screen in Kukátko where the app is meant to disappear and leave the
photograph. It has grown feature by feature and has never been looked at as a whole. This is
that look — after the settings dialog, repeat, shuffle and captions have landed.

**Runs after task `08b4c26e` (the start dialog, repeat, shuffle and captions).** Everything it
adds is in scope here as material; nothing it specifies should be re-litigated.

## Starting points (read from the code on 2026-08-12)

Not a defect list — the places worth looking at first, and what is already true so the pass does
not undo a deliberate decision.

- **The control bar never hides.** Its doc calls it "always-available": previous, play/pause,
  next, fullscreen, settings and close sit over the photo for the entire show. On a projector or
  a TV that is chrome competing with the picture for the whole evening. Whether it should fade
  on idle — and what then brings it back on a phone, where there is no mouse to move — is the
  single biggest question this pass has to answer.
- **Touch is one gesture.** A horizontal swipe past a minimum travel goes next/previous. There
  is no tap-to-pause, no way to reach the controls without them being permanently on screen, and
  nothing distinguishes a swipe from a scroll attempt.
- **Keyboard is arrows, space and Esc**, and Esc is overloaded: it leaves fullscreen or exits
  the show depending on state. Worth checking that is discoverable and that a keyboard-only user
  can reach every control, including the new dialog's.
- **Loading, empty and error states exist** (`Spinner`, `EmptyState`, `ErrorState` in
  `SlideshowPage.tsx`) — check they are right for a screen that is usually fullscreen and dark,
  not for a page inside the app's normal layout.
- **The page holds the advance until the next image has decoded**, and preloads a window of
  upcoming slides. That is deliberate and stops a flash of empty stage; do not "simplify" it.
- **Ken Burns already stands down under reduced motion**, as does any transition that would
  animate. Keep that, and extend it to whatever new motion the pass introduces.

## Requirements

- Go through the player as a whole — the control bar and when it should be visible, the
  transitions, the phone, keyboard and touch, fullscreen, and the loading, empty and error
  states — and fix what does not hold up.
- **Every change is justified out loud.** For each one, say what was wrong and how it was
  checked. "Looks better" is not a reason; a control that cannot be reached on a phone, a
  caption unreadable over a light photo, or a transition that stutters at 1 s intervals are.
- **Say what was deliberately left alone**, and why. A pass that reports only what it changed
  hides the judgement that matters most.
- The photograph wins every trade-off. Where chrome and picture compete for the same pixels, the
  picture takes them.
- Verify on a real phone viewport with a genuinely coarse pointer, not just a narrow window —
  the two are not the same and the app has been caught by that difference before.
- Nothing regresses: the settings, the captions, repeat, shuffle, preloading and the resume
  behaviour from the previous task all still work, and its tests still pass unchanged unless a
  change is deliberate and explained.

## Out of scope

- New features. If the pass turns up something the player lacks, write it down rather than
  building it — this task earns its keep by improving what is there.
- Music, export, casting, sharing.
- The photo detail page's own viewer, and the grids the slideshow starts from.

## Implementation notes

- `slideshow.css` holds the player's styling; the theme's surface tokens are the app's, and a
  fullscreen dark stage is one of the few places where the usual light/dark rules do not simply
  apply.
- The reduced-motion handling is already wired through a hook rather than a media query in CSS
  alone — follow it rather than adding a second mechanism.
- Screenshots of before and after, at a phone and a desktop viewport, are the cheapest way to
  make the report reviewable.
