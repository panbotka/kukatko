# Who is in this video

Two separate problems meet in the same panel, and both are confirmed on a live instance.

**A face detected on a video cannot be named.** Face detection *does* run on videos — the upload
pipeline enqueues it for every media type and the detector works on the video's poster frame. On
the staging instance six faces are recorded across two videos right now. The viewer even shows
them: the People block lists them as numbered chips, "#1 #2 #3". But clicking a chip navigates to
the faces panel and the page silently falls back to the info panel, because the faces view is
gated on the item being a still. The faces are visible, countable, and completely unreachable.

**A person with no detected face cannot be attached at all.** Only the poster frame is ever
looked at, so anyone who appears later in the clip is invisible to the detector — and on a photo
the detector can miss a profile, a back of a head or a face in a crowd just the same.

## Requirements

### Naming the faces a video already has

- The faces panel opens on a video and behaves as it does on a still: the detected faces are
  listed, each can be assigned to a subject, rejected as not-a-face, or marked as not that
  person, through the write paths that already exist. No new assignment mechanism.
- The face boxes are drawn over the poster frame. The poster is what the detector actually saw,
  so the geometry lines up; crop the boxes from the fitted thumbnail, not the square tile one,
  which is centre-cropped and would place them wrong.
- The overlay must not fight the video: it belongs on the poster, not on top of a playing clip.
  Showing it while the video is stopped, and getting out of the way once playback starts, is the
  behaviour to aim for.
- Clicking a numbered chip must reach the faces panel rather than silently falling back to info.
  If a state exists where the panel genuinely cannot open, the chip must not offer the click.

### Attaching a person by hand

The backend for this is a separate contract:

- The photo detail carries a `people` array — the subjects attached by hand, each with at least
  its uid, name and type. It is separate from the detected-face data.
- `POST /photos/{uid}/people` attaches a subject; `DELETE /photos/{uid}/people/{subject_uid}`
  detaches one. Both need the write role and answer with the resulting `people` array, so no
  re-fetch is needed.

If those endpoints are not there yet, do the detected-faces half, say clearly in the final report
that the hand-attach half was not done, and do not invent a different API shape.

- An "add person" control is available on a video, on a photo with no detected faces, and on a
  photo that does have them — someone in the background whose face was never detected needs
  naming too, so it is always offered, not only as a fallback.
- Choosing a person reuses the person picker the face-assignment flow already uses. Do not build
  a second picker.
- Hand-attached people appear as chips in the same People block, visually distinguishable from
  face chips: they have no crop to show, so they carry a generic person glyph. It must be obvious
  which names came from a detected face and which were added by hand.
- Each hand-attached chip has a remove control; removing updates the block from the response.
- A read-only viewer role sees every chip but gets neither the add nor the remove control.
- A failed write reports the error the way other curation writes in the viewer do, and leaves the
  list unchanged.

## Texts

Czech default, English second, both filled. A subject can be an animal, so prefer wording that
works for people and pets alike — "kdo je na videu" rather than anything assuming a human.

## Tests

The faces panel opening on a video and an assignment going through; a numbered chip's click
reaching the panel; the add control appearing in all three cases; add and remove updating from
the response; the read-only role seeing neither control. An open drawer or dialog is a portal —
query it by its dialog role rather than expecting a sibling in the tree.
