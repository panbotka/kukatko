# Near-duplicate detection judges a video by one frame

Videos take part in near-duplicate detection on exactly the same footing as photographs, and
nothing in the duplicate packages or the compare screen looks at media type. What a video
contributes is its poster: a perceptual hash and an embedding of a single frame grabbed a second
in. Two consequences follow, and the second one loses data.

**False groups.** Two unrelated clips that happen to open similarly — the same room, the same
dark intro, the same slate, two clips from one afternoon — look like near-duplicates because their
one sampled frame matches. So does a video against a photograph taken moments before it.

**A merge can archive the video.** Resolving a group keeps one item and archives the rest, with no
media-type check anywhere in the resolve path. Nothing stops a thirty-second clip being archived
in favour of a still photograph of the same scene, and the compare screen that is supposed to let
the user judge shows two pictures with no indication that one of them is a video with two minutes
of footage behind it. The user is asked to choose without being told what they are choosing
between.

## Requirements

- A group must never mix media kinds. A video and a still are not duplicates of each other
  whatever their frames look like, so pairs across that boundary are not offered at all. Two
  videos may legitimately be duplicates of each other — that case stays.
- The compare screen states what each candidate is: a video is marked as a video, with its
  duration, before any keep-or-archive decision is made. The same goes for the duplicates listing
  that leads into it.
- The resolve path refuses to archive a video in favour of a still, as a guard rather than a
  convention — if the situation somehow reaches it, it fails loudly instead of quietly archiving
  the clip.
- Consider whether a video's poster frame should feed duplicate detection at all. Judging two
  clips by one frame each is weak evidence; if the honest answer is that videos should only be
  compared with each other and only with more evidence than a single frame, say so in the
  documentation and keep the conservative behaviour rather than inventing a heavier comparison in
  this task.
- Whatever the rule ends up being, write it down where the duplicate detection is documented,
  including what a video contributes and what it does not.

## Tests

A video and a still with near-identical hashes producing no pair; two similar videos still
pairing; the compare payload carrying the media type and duration; the resolve guard refusing the
cross-kind archive. Include a regression case built from the real shape of the data — a poster
frame hash equal to a photograph's.
