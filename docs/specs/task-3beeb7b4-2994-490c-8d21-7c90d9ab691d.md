# The timeline rail collapses into an unreadable smear on the production library

`TimelineScrubber` (`web/src/components/library/TimelineScrubber.tsx`) renders one
tick per month bucket and one label per year boundary, each positioned at
`top: (bucket.cumulative / total) * 100%`. That works on a small library and falls
apart completely on the real one.

## Measured on production, 2026-08-02, at a 1280×633 viewport

```
rail height                    549 px
month ticks rendered           441
year labels rendered           103   (16 px tall each -> 1648 px of text)
adjacent year pairs overlapping 93 of 102
  ... most by the full 16 px, i.e. labels stacked exactly on top of each other
tick pairs closer than 2 px    368 of 440
smallest gap between ticks     0.02 px
```

Visually: 2026–2016 is a blur, and everything from 2004 downwards is a solid white
smear of overprinted text. The month ticks merge into one continuous bar.

## Why it happens

Two independent causes, both structural rather than cosmetic:

1. **Position is proportional to photo count, not to time.** `cumulative / total`
   maps the rail to the *scroll index*. The library holds 10 653 photos spanning
   **1905 to 2026** — 121 years — with the overwhelming majority in the last two
   decades. Every sparse historical year therefore collapses into a fraction of a
   pixel, and their labels land on top of one another.

2. **Nothing limits how many labels and ticks are drawn.** Every month bucket gets a
   tick and every year boundary gets a label, however many pixels are available.
   441 ticks cannot be distinguished in 549 px, and 103 labels need three times the
   rail's height.

The data is genuine, not a defect: the oldest years hold real scanned photos in
plausible counts (1905: 7, 1910: 11, 1912: 2 …), there are **no** photos with a
missing date, and no pile-up on a sentinel date. The component has to handle a real
long-tailed archive.

This never showed in development because a small library produces few buckets.

## What to build

Rework the rail so it stays legible at any library size and shape. The design is
yours, but these are the constraints that make it non-trivial:

- **Positioning must keep matching the grid's scroll mapping.** The rail jumps by
  passing `bucket.cumulative` as a photo index (`onJump`), and drag maps a fraction
  of the rail height back through `bucketForIndex`. If you make positions linear in
  *time* instead of in photo count, the two stop agreeing and every drag lands in the
  wrong month. Either keep density-proportional positions and thin the *labels*, or
  change the jump mapping to match — but do not change one without the other. Say in
  the commit message which you chose and why.
- **Draw only what fits.** Decide labels and ticks from the rail's measured height:
  emit a label only when it clears the previous one by a minimum gap, and collapse
  month ticks that cannot be told apart. A rail that shows a dozen readable years is
  worth more than one showing all 103 illegibly.
- **Sparse eras must stay reachable.** Whatever thinning you apply, a user must still
  be able to get to 1905. If density-proportional positioning makes the first sixty
  years a few pixels tall, that is a usability problem in its own right — solve it or
  state explicitly why it is acceptable.
- **Do not regress the fix already in the CSS.** `.kukatko-timeline-current` (the
  floating month bubble) deliberately lives in its own lane left of the rail so it
  cannot collide with the year labels — see the comments at `app.css:996-1002` and
  `1059-1065`. That collision was fixed once; keep it fixed.
- The rail is already hidden below a small-screen breakpoint; that stays.

## Verification — this is the part that matters

The existing component test passes today while production is unreadable, so a green
suite proves nothing here.

- Add a test over a **realistic** bucket set: ~440 month buckets spanning ~120 years
  with a strongly skewed distribution (a handful of photos per year before 2000,
  thousands per year after 2010). Assert the invariant directly — **no two rendered
  labels overlap** — rather than asserting a particular label count.
- jsdom gives every element a zero-height box, so an overlap assertion there is
  meaningless unless you supply geometry. Verify for real in a browser against a
  throwaway instance on the test database, or with a static harness that loads the
  real Bootswatch + `app.css`, and measure the rendered boxes. Report the same
  numbers as above (labels drawn, overlapping pairs, smallest gap) as evidence.
- `make check` must pass (ESLint strict, Prettier, Vitest).

## Production constraints

Kukátko runs live at https://kukatko.kotrzina.cz against the real library.

- This task is **frontend only** — no migration, no backend change should be needed.
- **Do not touch the production instance, its database or its bucket**, and do not
  run `make dev` (it binds the port of the developer's own running instance).
- An import may be running while this is reviewed; the timeline endpoint is read-only,
  so work against a throwaway instance rather than anything live.
