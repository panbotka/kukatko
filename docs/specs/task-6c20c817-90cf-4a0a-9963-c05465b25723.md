# "The video is still being prepared" instead of a dead end

Uploading a video offers a play button immediately. If the streaming encode has not run yet, the
player falls back to the progressive endpoint serving the original file — and when the browser
cannot decode that codec, the user lands on a permanent-looking "this video cannot be played,
download it instead" panel. Nothing tells them that the encode which would make it playable is
sitting in the queue, or that waiting a few minutes would fix it.

## What the client already has

The photo detail response carries two things this needs, and the player consults neither:

- `hls` (boolean) — whether the clip has at least one encoded streaming rendition. Today this is
  the only signal the player gets.
- `processing` — one entry per per-photo computation, each `{step, state, at?, error?}`. One of
  the steps is `hls_transcode`, and its state is one of `done`, `running`, `queued`, `failed`,
  `skipped` or `pending`. `skipped` means the encode does not apply — streaming is switched off
  instance-wide, or the item is not a standalone video.

## Bug to fix in the same task

The backend added the `hls_transcode` step to the processing report, but the frontend's step
union and the `photo.processing.steps.*` translation block (Czech and English) never got it. The
Processing panel translates the step name straight from the step id, so **every photo in the
library currently renders a row reading the literal string `photo.processing.steps.hls_transcode`**.
Add the step to the frontend union and give it a proper Czech and English label describing what
it is — preparing the video for smooth playback and seeking, not "HLS transcode".

## Requirements

- Playback that works today must keep working. A browser-decodable original still plays
  progressively while its encode is queued. Do not gate playback on the encode.
- When playback is genuinely not possible, the panel that replaces the player is state-aware:
  - `queued` or `pending` — the video is still being prepared for playback, come back in a
    moment; keep offering the download link.
  - `running` — the same, worded as being processed right now.
  - `failed` — preparation failed; the error is surfaced, and the reader is pointed at the
    Processing panel, where a maintainer already has a "run now" control for the step.
  - `skipped` or `done` — the existing "this browser cannot play this video, download it"
    message, unchanged. This is the case where streaming is switched off for the whole instance,
    or where the clip was encoded and still cannot be decoded.
- While the clip has no rendition yet and the encode is `queued` or `running`, the viewer shows a
  quiet, non-blocking hint even when progressive playback works — a small badge or one line under
  the player, never a modal — so the user understands why seeking is coarse and that a better
  version is coming.
- While the encode is `queued` or `running` and the viewer is open on that clip, the detail is
  re-checked periodically (every ten to twenty seconds) so the player switches to the streaming
  source as soon as it lands, without a page reload. Polling stops when the step reaches a
  terminal state, when the viewer moves to another item, and when the tab is hidden. It must not
  fire for a photo, for a video whose encode is already done, or for one where the step is
  skipped — an instance with streaming switched off must generate no extra traffic at all.
- Nothing about a still photo's viewer changes.

## Texts

Czech default, English second, both filled. The wording is for a family archive, not an
operations console: say the video is being prepared for playing, not that a transcode job is
enqueued.

## Tests

Component tests over the player for each of the states above — including that a decodable clip
with a queued encode still plays, that the poll starts only in the queued/running case and stops
on a terminal state, and that the Processing panel renders a real label for the streaming step
rather than a raw key. Fake timers in this project need the option that lets time advance for
real, or async queries hang.
