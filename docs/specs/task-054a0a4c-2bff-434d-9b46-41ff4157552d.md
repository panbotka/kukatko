# The upload result says what was actually uploaded

Upload a video and the page congratulates you on uploading a photo. The whole upload flow is
worded as if the library held nothing but stills: the closing sentence counts "photos", and so do
the lines around it about albums and labels. The only place that acknowledges video exists is the
file picker's accessible label.

## Requirements

- The closing sentence of a finished upload reports photos and videos separately, by count: a
  batch of three stills and one clip reads as three photos and one video, a clip on its own reads
  as one video, and a batch of stills only keeps today's wording exactly.
- The same applies to the variant of that sentence used when the batch was filed into an album or
  given labels.
- The surrounding copy stops assuming stills. The line saying the batch is in no album, the line
  during upload about choosing where these belong, and the hint that a choice applies to
  everything in the batch must all read correctly for a batch that is videos only or mixed. Where
  no count is involved, wording that covers both is enough; where the batch is stills only, keep
  the wording that is there.
- The duplicate and failed counters keep their present meaning and wording. They count files, and
  a duplicate video and a duplicate photo are the same event.
- Czech is the default locale and needs the complete plural set for both nouns, in the right
  grammatical form for the joined sentence — this is the part to get right, and a machine-shaped
  "3 fotek a 1 video" is not acceptable. English second.

## No backend change is needed

The classification is already available on the client: the upload queue holds the picked file,
and there is a helper that decides image, video or neither from the extension and the MIME type —
the upload list already uses it to swap the thumbnail placeholder to a play glyph. Count with
that over the settled items. The per-file result from the server carries no media type and does
not need one.

## Tests

Component tests over the finished stage for a stills-only batch, a videos-only batch and a mixed
batch, in both locales, asserting the rendered sentence rather than the translation key.
